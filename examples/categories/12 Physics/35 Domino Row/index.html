Domino Row

Line up thin boxes as dominoes and topple the first one with Arashtad 3D Runtime and Havok physics.

Physics Multiple Chain
8 thin dominoes · click the small cube to start the chain

What this demonstrates

A chain reaction between many dynamic bodies, driven by the physics of toppling contacts and shared ground.

Domino Geometry

Each domino is a thin box with width="0.3", height="1.4", and depth="0.8". Their tall thin shape makes them easy to topple while still stable before the impact.

Chain Reaction

Dominoes are spaced 1 unit apart in X, so when one falls its top reaches the next one's top and knocks it over. One push propagates down the row.

Source

Eight thin boxes in a row, plus a small pusher that can be launched with a click. 

<arashtad-mesh
    id="d1"
    type="Box"
    width="0.3"
    height="1.4"
    depth="0.8"
    position="new BABYLON.Vector3(-4.5, 0.7, 0)"
    material="green"
>
    <arashtad-physics
        type="BOX"
        mass="1"
        restitution="0.05"
        friction="0.6"
    ></arashtad-physics>
</arashtad-mesh>

<!-- Repeat d2..d8 at x=-3.5, -2.5, -1.5, -0.5, 0.5, 1.5, 2.5 -->

<arashtad-mesh
    id="pusher"
    type="Box"
    size="0.5"
    position="new BABYLON.Vector3(-6, 0.5, 0)"
    material="brown"
    onPickTrigger="
        const aggregate = target.metadata?.physicsAggregate;
        aggregate?.body?.setLinearVelocity(
            new BABYLON.Vector3(6, 0, 0)
        );
    "
>
    <arashtad-physics
        type="BOX"
        mass="2"
        restitution="0.1"
        friction="0.4"
    ></arashtad-physics>
</arashtad-mesh>
